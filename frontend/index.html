Punjab Agronomic Reference & Data Methodology

Understanding varietal trade-offs, groundwater sustainability, and how AgriYield Punjab computes district forecasts.

1. PR 126 vs. Long-Duration Pusa Lines (Chitta / Peeli / Dogar)

While farmer-preferred lines like Chitta Pusa, Peeli Pusa (Pusa 44), and Dogar Pusa routinely yield 34–38 q/ekad on heavy tubewell soils, they require 150–165 days seed-to-seed and 22–28% more irrigation cycles—accelerating groundwater depletion across Central and Malwa dark zones and delaying November wheat/potato sowing. By contrast, PR 126 matures in ~123 days total (~93 days post-transplanting) when transplanted at the strict 30-day nursery stage, saving 4–5 irrigations and clearing fields early for timely Rabi sowing.

2. Milled Rice vs. Raw Paddy (Dhan) Conversion

Official Directorate of Economics & Statistics (DES) Punjab crop tables report rice yield in terms of milled rice after hulling. Because standard paddy milling yields approximately 67% head/broken rice (1 quintal raw paddy = 67 kg rice), AgriYield Punjab divides raw model output by 0.67 for Kharif Rice so farmers see actual trolley-weight paddy yields matching mandi weighbridges.

3. Multi-Crop Rotation & Soil Health in Punjab

Intensive paddy–wheat monoculture depletes soil zinc, iron, and organic carbon. Incorporating short-duration Summer Moong (SML 1827 / SML 832, 60–65 days) after wheat harvest adds biological nitrogen fixation, while pairing early-maturing paddy or Basmati (Pusa 1509 / PR 126) enables profitable autumn Potato followed by late-sown PBW 752 wheat.
